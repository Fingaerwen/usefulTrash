usefulTrash privacy policy

Effective 28 October 2026.

usefulTrash is a pair of personal apps made by Fingaerwen for their own use: Talon, a gym tracker (called Gym Tracker until version 0.4), and Ambrosia, a diet tracker. Each is an Android app with a companion web page, its "PC view". Both use the same sign-in and the same database. Sign-ups are closed: only the owner's account can sign in. This page explains what data the apps handle, including data they receive from Google.

Data the apps handle

  • What you enter in Talon: your training plan, workouts, body weight (and the time you saved a weigh-in), daily step totals and notes.
  • What you enter in Ambrosia: what you eat (foods, amounts and meals), the water you drink, your nutrition targets and the changes you apply to them. Ambrosia reads your training plan, workouts and body weight from Talon's data.
  • From Google when you sign in: your email address, name, profile picture and Google account ID.
  • Sign-in tokens that keep you signed in.

The camera and food lookups

  • Ambrosia uses the phone's camera only to scan a food's barcode, and only while you scan. The scanning happens on the phone: no picture is kept or sent anywhere.
  • To look up a packaged food, Ambrosia sends only its barcode, or the words you search for, to Open Food Facts, a free and open food database. Nothing about you goes with it.

How Google user data is used

  • Sign in with Google (your email, name and profile picture): Google's sign-in token is passed to Supabase, the app's sign-in and database service, to sign you in to your own account. It's used for nothing else.
  • Google Drive (only "files this app created"): each app saves backup copies of its own data in its own folder in your Google Drive and deletes its own older backups (it keeps the last 3). Neither can see, open or change any other file in your Drive. On the phone, Google Play services keeps this permission. The apps only receive access tokens that expire after about an hour, keep them in memory, and send them only to Google. A PC view asks Google for Drive access each time you press its backup button.

Where data is stored

  • On your phone: your data, in each app's private storage. Sign-in tokens are encrypted with a key kept in the phone's security hardware (Android Keystore) and are left out of Android's backups.
  • In Supabase (database hosting): your data and your account record (the Google details above). Row-level security lets only your own signed-in account read or change your data.
  • In your Google Drive: the backup files the apps created.
  • The PC views keep their sign-in in the browser tab's memory only: closing the tab signs you out. They store nothing in your browser, apart from handing Google's Drive permission to the PC view that asked for it, which removes it at once.

Sharing

No data is sold, rented or shared with anyone. There are no ads, analytics or trackers. The only services involved are Supabase (sign-in and database), Google (sign-in and Drive) and Open Food Facts (food lookups, which get only a barcode or search words), and only to provide the features above.

Limited Use

usefulTrash's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your choices and deletion

  • Remove usefulTrash's access to your Google account at any time in your Google Account: Your connections to third-party apps & services.
  • Backups are ordinary files in your Google Drive: delete them there whenever you like.
  • Uninstalling an app deletes the data it stored on your phone.
  • To have your account and data in Supabase deleted, open an issue on the project's GitHub page (below).

Contact

Questions or requests: open an issue at github.com/Fingaerwen/usefulTrash/issues.

Changes

Changes to this policy are published on this page with a new effective date.
